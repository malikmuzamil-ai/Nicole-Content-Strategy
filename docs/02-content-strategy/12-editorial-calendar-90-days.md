## The first thirty days, in one line

Twelve posts, three a week, **all image led. No text posts anywhere this month.** One cheatsheet
opens week one, one carousel closes every week after that, two image posts fill out the rest of
each week.

**Zero Selling intent posts in the first thirty days.** Every post here is Education, Trust or
Authority. The first application facing line does not appear until month two. **The job this month
is voice first.**

## Week 1 · Establish the voice

Image post, image post, cheatsheet.

### 1.1 · Make Me Think · Image Post

> **Delegation is an act of ego.**

**Angle.** Six words. Deliberately the shortest, hardest card in the set, no elaboration
underneath it. The reader supplies her own examples.

**Context.** Reframes over-control as self-importance rather than competence, which lands
precisely because it contradicts how the reader has been praised her whole career: for being the
one who can do it all.

**Source.** Founder essay on delegation and ego (Food for Founders).

### 1.2 · Understand Me · Image Post

> **Rich, but unknown. Or worse: unknown, and unnecessary.**

**Angle.** Two short fragments, a hard pause between them, built to be read twice.

**Context.** Describes precisely what founders report feeling once they are financially secure but
no longer central to anything. The exact isolation her own gap table names as *people all around
them, and no one who actually knows them.*

**Source.** "The Post-Exit Void," founder identity essay.

### 1.3 · Teach Me · Cheatsheet

> **The question that made him go quiet.**

**Angle.** Open on the scene, not the framework. A client once listed everything he had out loud:
the business, the money, the marriage, the health. She asked what he had of himself. He did not
answer for a long time. **That pause becomes the cheatsheet.**

**Context.** Five rows, one honest answer each. Success against fulfilment. Performance against
feeling alive. A role against real identity. Hustle and safety against calling. People around them
against anyone who actually knows them. Opens the month on the single most saveable asset in the
plan, built from an actual moment rather than a listicle.

**Source.** Nicole's own five gap structure, 11 August positioning brief, reframed narratively.

## Week 2 · Hold the register

Image post, image post, carousel.

### 2.1 · Make Me Think · Image Post

> **"I was introduced as someone's daughter. I was not myself."**

**Angle.** A direct quote, attributed generally to a leader who inherited a family business. The
card carries the line alone, no annotation.

**Context.** Says in eight words what an entire pillar of this positioning is built to address:
the founder who built or inherited something and still has not been asked who she is underneath
it.

**Source.** South China Morning Post interview, a third generation leader of a jewellery company.
Public figure, publicly reported, used here as general pattern rather than a named case.

### 2.2 · Entertain Me · Image Post

> **In some cultures, the cure for a burn is more heat, not water. It works.**

**Angle.** Her own bridging mechanism exactly as she described it: a physical, sensory detail from
somewhere she has actually been, paired with the emotional pattern it mirrors.

**Context.** Grief runs on the same logic, and almost nobody tests it before reaching for the
water anyway. Reconstructs the mechanic she described herself, without inventing a specific
unverified country or spice detail.

**Source.** Nicole's own description of the Entertain Me format, 5 August session.

### 2.3 · Teach Me · Carousel

> **Trust factors named most often at this altitude.**

**Angle.** A short, specific list rather than a provocation. Useful as a save-worthy "what to look
for" post: somebody who has actually been in the room, discretion under real stakes, a presence
that does not flinch at what surfaces.

**Context.** Synthesises the trust factors already documented in her own client profile with what
founder coaching research independently identifies as the actual criteria accomplished people use
when choosing an advisor, distinct from credentials or price.

**Source.** Her own Ideal Customer Profile document, cross checked against executive coaching
trust research.

## Week 3 · Widen the proof

Image post, image post, carousel.

### 3.1 · Understand Me · Image Post

> **The calendars that were packed now lie eerily blank. And so does the sense of who you are.**

**Angle.** A quiet, visual image, the empty calendar, rather than a direct emotional statement.
Let the reader supply the feeling.

**Context.** Almost verbatim from post-exit founder writing describing the days right after a sale
or handover: the external structure disappears overnight, and the identity that depended on it
disappears with it.

**Source.** "The Post-Exit Void," founder identity essay.

### 3.2 · Entertain Me · Image Post

> **One founder sold his company for half a billion dollars and, months later, asked out loud: how
> much golf can I play?**

**Angle.** Story first, punchline last. The absurdity of the question is the point. It makes an
abstract feeling concrete, and a little funny, before it turns serious.

**Context.** A real, publicly reported case, a founder's own words after a major exit. One of the
strongest available story hooks: it disarms with humour before it lands the actual point.

**Source.** Widely reported founder interview following a major acquisition.

### 3.3 · Teach Me · Carousel

> **The most fundamental part of succession is replacing yourself. Almost nobody plans for that.**

**Angle.** States a hard truth about planning most owners genuinely have not confronted, framed as
an omission rather than a failure.

**Context.** Grounded in succession planning writing that treats replacing yourself as the real,
usually skipped task, distinct from the legal and financial planning most owners assume is the
whole job.

**Source.** **Amber, struck 21 September.** This card originally carried "only 30 percent of family
businesses survive to the second generation", attributed to Family Business Institute research. The
trail does not resolve and it fails doc 07 rule three, so it came off the proof floor in doc 03 and
no post is drafted against it. The candidate replacement is McKinsey, February 2026: total
shareholder return down an average 5.7 points in the five years after a family CEO transition,
across 200 listed family businesses in forty plus countries. **It needs her written yes first.**
Until she rules, the slot holds and the line publishes without a number or not at all. A missed
slot costs one day.

## Week 4 · Close the month

Image post, image post, carousel.

### 4.1 · Make Me Think · Image Post

> **Insecure leaders avoid delegating, not because others cannot do the job, but because they fear
> being overshadowed and rendered obsolete.**

**Angle.** A single, uncomfortable diagnostic sentence. No softening, no "but that's okay"
underneath it.

**Context.** Reframes a founder's white knuckle grip on the business as fear dressed up as
diligence, exactly the register this positioning calls for: naming the thing nobody else will say.

**Source.** Leadership psychology research on delegation avoidance.

### 4.2 · Understand Me · Image Post

> **You believe a different future is available to other people. Just not to you.**

**Angle.** Second person, direct, no softening. The gap between what she would tell a friend and
what she believes about herself.

**Context.** This is the belief test from her own offer document, restated as recognition rather
than as a screening question. The person she turns away does not believe change is available at
all. The person she is for believes it, but only for other people, not herself yet.

**Source.** Nicole's own positioning brief, 11 August.

### 4.3 · Teach Me · Carousel

> **Executives who delegate effectively generate roughly a third more revenue than those who
> cannot let go.**

**Angle.** Opens on a number, which earns its place specifically because it is genuinely useful,
citable information, not just an emotional hook.

**Context.** The rare purely educational card in the month, the kind a reader saves because it is
simply a fact she did not have. Closes the month on hard evidence rather than a feeling, and sets
up month two without selling anything yet.

**Source.** **Amber, flagged 21 September.** The roughly one third figure is carried everywhere as
Harvard Business Review research and the trail we hold does not reach a Harvard Business Review
study. Doc 07 rule three wants the institution that actually ran it. Held until the primary study is
named, and the card does not open on a number until then.

### Month two, the shape

Twelve posts. Two Selling intent posts at most, both in the second half of the month, both
preceded by two Authority or Education posts in the same week. Format mix opens up: four text
posts, four image posts, two carousels, two cheatsheets.

The first Selling intent post is the only genuinely new thing this month. It does not pitch. It
names the Assess once, in her own first comment, at the end of a post that has already earned it.

### Month three, the shape

Twelve posts. One video test inside Entertain Me, evaluated against the bars in doc 20 rather than
against how it felt. Everything else runs as month two. By day 90 the calendar should be planned
four weeks ahead at all times, not two.
