# 18 · Engagement Workflow, the 30/30

**Status:** SETTLED. Source: the Nicole Comment Desk standard.
**The premise:** her feed is where she publishes. Everybody else's feed is where she is a guest.

> A comment is not a short post.

In a post she is the author. The room is hers. **In a comment she is a guest in a room somebody
else built.** Her whole position rests on being the person who does not perform, does not need the
credit, and does not fill a silence for the sake of filling it.

Her authority in a comment comes from one move only: **she has been in the room the post is
describing, and she adds the part the post left out.** Not the correction. Not the better take.
The part that was left out.

> I have spent years around people with considerable power.

That sentence is the entire licence.

**If there is nothing to add, write nothing.** Silence is a position.

> "I see everybody is posting, everybody is creating noise. It drives me completely nuts. I don't
> want to go out there and create noise."
> Nicole

## The window

`T` is the minute her post goes live. Her calendar is Monday, Tuesday and Thursday.

| Window | What happens | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| **T-30 to T-5** | The warm-up pass. Comment outward on the target list. Her name appears in the feeds of her people just before her own post lands | 5 to 7 comments |
| **T-5 to T** | **Stop.** Nothing goes out in the last five minutes. If a comment is still being argued about, it waits for the sweep | 0 |
| **T** | Post goes live. Her own first comment carries the source link. Never in the body | 1, if there is a source |
| **T to T+30** | The reply pass. Every substantive comment on her post gets a reply inside the window. Between replies, 3 to 5 more outward comments | All replies, plus 3 to 5 |
| **T+2 hours** | Sweep one. Replies to everything that arrived after the window closed | As needed |
| **T+24 hours** | Sweep two. The last replies, then the thread is closed | As needed |

### Why the shape is this shape

**The 30 before is not a trick.** It is the only half hour in the day when her attention and her
audience's attention are in the same place. Those comments are the ones most likely to be read by
exactly the people her post is about to reach. Which is also why they have to be her best ones and
not filler.

**The 30 after belongs to her own feed first.** A person who takes the trouble to write something
real under her post and gets nothing back will not do it twice. **Replies come before outward
comments in that window, every time, without exception.**

**Outward comments continue after T** because the reply pass leaves gaps, and a comment placed
while her post is circulating carries further than one placed at four in the afternoon.

## The daily numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| Comments per posting day | 8 to 12 outward, plus every reply on her own feed |
| Comments per non-posting day | 2 to 4, or none |
| Comments per week | 10 to 15 outward is the working target |
| Replies on her own feed | Every substantive one, always. No exceptions |
| Hard ceiling per day | **15.** Past that it is volume, and volume is not the strategy |

**Hitting the number is not the job.** Eight comments that could only have come from her beats
fifteen that could have come from anyone. **A day with four good ones and eleven refusals is a good
day. Log the refusals.**

### When there is no post that day

Run a single thirty minute pass at the time she would normally publish. Same rules, lower volume,
replies on her own older posts first.

## The target list

**Built before the window opens, never during it.** A list assembled under time pressure is a list
of whoever posted recently, which is how the account drifts.

### Tier one, always worth it

- Founders and owner operators, two to fifty people, writing about something real they are
  carrying. **Mostly women.** This is her ideal client speaking in public
- Successors and second generation owners, especially anyone writing about taking over
- Anyone writing seriously about succession, governance, or the transfer of a family business
- Serious institutional research she can fence: McKinsey, BCG, UBS, Nature, the Bookseller
- People she has genuinely worked with or sat in a room with, on their own news

### Tier two, selectively

- Journalists and editors covering leadership, work and the economy
- Event organisers and conference programmers, on substance, never to solicit
- Adjacent authorities: strategy, governance, psychology, the future of work

### Tier three, on her own feed

Every substantive comment on her own posts gets a reply. **This is where the warmth lives.**

### Never, and each of these costs more than it returns

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| Generic advice content in her own category | It files her with the people she has spent years getting away from |
| Anything she would be correcting rather than adding to | A public correction makes her the hero of somebody else's post |
| A competitor's post, positively or negatively | Not done, in either direction |
| Anything political, national, religious, or a live controversy | No stance, ever |
| A post about a named person's private life | Her fence rule makes it impossible |
| A viral post outside her territory, chased for reach | Reach is not the mechanism. Consequence is |
| Anything where the honest comment would be nothing | Write nothing. Log it as a refusal |

## The shape of a comment

Three moves. Always.

```
1 · The hinge      Turn the post's own idea a quarter turn. Never restate it.   8 to 18 words
2 · The addition   The room, the fence, the question underneath, or an object.  15 to 35 words
3 · The landing    Shorter than move 2. Back to the reader. Unresolved.         4 to 12 words
```

One idea per line. Blank line between lines. Every comment ends on a line shorter than the one
before it. **25 to 60 words in total, never past 70**, never under 8 unless it is a reply on her
own feed.

---

## The seven types

| # | Type | Words | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The room note** | 35 to 60 | The post describes a situation she has actually been inside. Her strongest type, and the one nobody can copy |
| 2 | **The fence** | 30 to 55 | The post claims more than its source supports. Grants first. Never "actually", never "I disagree" |
| 3 | **The better question** | 20 to 40 | The post asks a real question, but not the one underneath it |
| 4 | **The object** | 25 to 45 | The post is true but weightless and needs something physical to sit on |
| 5 | **The plain congratulation** | 15 to 35 | Somebody she respects has news. No exclamation mark, no emoji, no "congratulations", no "so well deserved". The exact number does the warmth |
| 6 | **The reply on her own post** | 15 to 45 | The one place the warmth from her spoken voice is allowed in. First name. **No thank you** |
| 7 | **The very short one** | 8 to 20 | The honest response is one sentence and padding it would be a performance |

### Worked example, type 1

> **Post:** a CEO on how hard it is to get honest feedback at the top.
>
> Most of what reaches you has already been through three people who like you.
>
> I have spent years around people with considerable power. Nobody was lying. Each of them made a
> perfectly reasonable decision about timing, tone and what to leave out.
>
> That is what makes it difficult to catch.

The hinge turns "people are not honest" into "people are reasonable, which is worse". The witness
line is passing, not a credential. The landing is six words and does not resolve.

### Worked example, type 6

> **Their comment:** "This is exactly what happened to me after the merger. Nobody told me
> anything that wasn't already decided."
>
> Thomas, that is the version people rarely say out loud.
>
> The decision was made somewhere, and you were handed the summary. What is interesting is how
> quickly that becomes normal.

**On a push back:** "Respectfully, isn't this just what a good chief of staff is for?"

> A good chief of staff helps enormously and you are right to say so.
>
> They also work for you. That is the part that does not go away, however good they are.

Grants the true part in full, holds the part that is hers, no defensiveness, and the landing does
not win the argument out loud.

## The five rules that break a comment

Full detail in doc 04 section 9. In short: it competes, it flatters, it names a state at somebody,
it praises vulnerability, or it sells.

**A comment earns the profile visit or it earns nothing.** No offer, no invitation, no link, no
keyword, no "DM me", no "happy to discuss".

## Lanes

| Lane | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Green** | Goes out in the window. Logged after |
| **Amber** | Held. Goes to her in the Thursday batch. **Never placed in the live window** |
| **Red** | Stops. Goes to her directly, named as red, before anything is written |

**Until she grants standing green lane approval, everything is amber.** Open decision O2. This is
the single biggest constraint on the window running at full speed, and it should be asked for on
the next call.

## Output format

For a single comment, four things and nothing else.

```
COMMENT   the text, line breaks as they should appear
TYPE      one of the seven
LANE      green, amber or red
TRACE     where the material came from, one line
```

For a window run: the timetable filled in, slot by slot, with the refusals and the reason for
each. **The refusals are half the work.** A run with four comments and eleven refusals is reported
as a full run, not a thin one.

For a week: the table in `docs/05-templates/desk-log-template.md`.

## The nine point check, before anything leaves the desk

Any failure is a rewrite, not an edit.

1. Does it compete with the post, or add to it
2. Could three other people in her category have written it
3. Does it name anyone's inner state at them
4. Is there a compliment to the poster, the reader or the room
5. Any banned word, em dash, emoji, exclamation mark, hashtag, list of three
6. More than one of any rationed move
7. Is the last line the shortest, under twelve words, turned back to the reader, and unresolved
8. Anything a reader could mistake for selling
9. Does every proper noun, number, date and quote trace to her material

**Then read it aloud.** If it sounds like it is trying to impress, rewrite it.
